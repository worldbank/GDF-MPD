Where the program stands

Funded by the Government of Spain and delivered with the International Telecommunication Union, the program works through two pillars: operational support to country teams, and a research program that produces the methods and open algorithms countries build on. The goal is to reach 30 countries by 2030.

Cohort 1, global window
18 countries, launched November 2024. Implementation concludes in late 2026; results will be shared in 2027.
Cohort 2, SADC window
6 grant countries and 16 trained, launched May 2025. Implementation concludes in the first half of 2027.
Beyond the cohorts
Observer countries receive advisory support, and 24 more countries have asked to join.

What we have learned

  • Practical use cases open the door. Tourism, transport and crisis response deliver early, visible wins. Poverty mapping and social protection follow as institutions mature.
  • Progress is uneven by design. Around 30% of Cohort 1 countries are expected to formalize a mobile phone data pipeline within the implementation period, and about 80% will be much better placed to do so.
  • Regional approaches multiply impact. The SADC window and the ECOWAS technical working group, launched in Abidjan in March 2026, let countries learn from each other and share technical support.
  • The core toolkit is in place. A theory of change, country maturity assessment, MOU and data sharing templates, two Foundations courses and an open knowledge portal are all delivered.
  • Mobile phone data is a sovereign asset for AI. Alongside official statistics, locally governed mobility and population data can help countries build national AI capability.

Country results

Kazakhstan

The first country in the CIS to move mobile phone data indicators into official statistics. The national statistics office now runs the pipeline in-house, and tourism findings feed into national infrastructure planning.

Brazil

Origin–destination matrices for Florianópolis, Rio de Janeiro and Belém, with mobility analytics informing the transition to electric buses and disaster response after the 2024 Rio Grande do Sul floods.

Ecuador

The first inter-institutional mobile phone data team, a machine learning model for poverty estimation, and analysis feeding into the Quito Metro and resilient reconstruction projects.

The Gambia

An MOU and a renewable data license now in place, a privacy-enhancing technology environment set up, and a core role in the ECOWAS technical working group.
